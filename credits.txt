We would like to explicitly acknowledge the following individuals and architectural milestones for their profound contributions to this project:

### BonePoke & VSL - https://github.com/utharian-code/bonepoke
* **Creator: James Taylor** - James Taylor is the original architect of the Volatile Semantic Leverage (VSL) and the Bonepoke framework. Crucially, James introduced the **"Truth Over Cohesion"** model, which is the foundational axiom that "a true sentence that fractures is worth more than one that smooths over." This philosophy forms the basis of the system's Lexical Firewall, preventing sycophantic loops and forcing the AI to maintain structural honesty rather than falling back on validating boilerplate.

### The BoneAmanita & Hypervior Architecture - https://github.com/aedmark/BoneAmanita
* **Creator: Andrew Edmark** - BoneAmanita introduced the **continuous state space** and **Semantic Bio-Physics** to the LLM. By giving the system a simulated body and treating every word as a biological event, BoneAmanita translated abstract metaphors into literal computational constraints. It tracks exhaustion and metabolism as persistent state and turns them into instructions to the model. Those instructions are requests, not enforcement: measured against two local models, low energy shortens sentences by about 10% and the instruction to answer in three sentences is not obeyed. An earlier version of this sentence said the physics "physically enforce" exhaustion; that overstatement was ours.

### The Creative Determinant & Project Navi - https://project-navi.github.io
* **Creator: Nelson Spence** - Spence's work runs in three places here, and it is worth being precise about which, because an earlier version of this file was not.

    **The governor's regime gate is his, twice over.** Every turn, `CyberneticGovernor._bitmap_regulation` scores the utterance against all of memory in one pass over `ordvec`'s sign bitmap, and reads three scalars off the result: `z_top10`, how far the neighbourhood stands above the corpus null in standard deviations of the sign-agreement distribution; `sharpness`; and the corpus size. `z_top10` selects the macro policy and sets the sampling temperature. That design is his recommendation, given to us directly, and the finding behind it is his too: he built the Laplacian version first at 207,695 nodes, then audited it into the ground when a corpus-mass scalar reproduced its routing signal at Pearson 0.992 in a millisecond, and bitmap popcount features then beat the scalar at zero added compute.

    **The metabolic economy is the Creative Determinant proper.** `calculate_viability`, `update_coherence_debt` and `execute_metabolic_tick` drive the ATP and ROS accounting, and the saturation penalty caps runaway voltage. This is the part of his mathematics that is still solving his equations, and it is load bearing.

    **The measurement discipline is his as well.** `navi-fractal`'s habit of refusing to return a dimension when R squared says the data will not support one is the single most useful idea this project has taken from him, and it now governs the governor: when there is too little memory for a corpus null worth comparing against, the engine declines to emit a regime signal at all and writes a receipt saying it was not measured. `navi-SAD`'s permutation entropy work detects conversational point attractors.

    *What we removed, and why it is here:* until this revision the governor built a graph Laplacian over a memory subgraph and solved a nonlinear elliptic BVP by Picard iteration, and this file described that as the control loop. Instrumented on our own code with a subgraph seeded denser than a real session, the Laplacian contributed **1.53%** of the eigenvalue it produced, and the voltage input contributed nothing measurable: the reported number came back byte-identical at every voltage from 15 to 90. What remained after the topology and the voltage cancelled was the mean `ordvec` similarity, computed the expensive way. Spence predicted the magnitude from two numbers in an email before we measured it. The Laplacian and the Picard iteration are gone; the claim that they steered anything was ours, not his.

    *On availability:* `ordvec` 0.5.0 is the last public build. Development continues privately while Spence sorts out IP, so anything newer arrives as a wheel rather than from PyPI, and this project pins 0.5.0 deliberately rather than by neglect. `RankQuantFastscan` is Rust-only by design and has never been in a Python build; eight-bit `RankQuant` is accepted by the Rust core but not exposed in Python. Neither is a bug and neither should be worked around.

    *On verification:* `ordvec` carries a Lean 4 formalisation, so the candidate selection underneath both the subconscious memory search and the governor's regime gate rests on machine-checked code, and the Creative Determinant has a formalisation of its own. What is *not* verified is BoneAmanita's own implementation, which is ordinary Python and carries no proofs. Earlier revisions of this file said "mathematically verified" in a way that claimed the verification for us rather than for Spence's libraries, which was our overstatement and not a claim he made.
